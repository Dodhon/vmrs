# Special Instructions for Completing the Repair Order Form (Form 6)

#### (When Used to Record Towing Activity)

Repair Order forms may be used to assign personnel and equipment to perform towing. This enables data collection for properly identifying towing expense to each piece of equipment so moved by an equipment operator.

Towing may be accomplished by companyowned or vendor-owned equipment under the following conditions:

- From one company site to another
- From field to company site
- From field to vendor site
- From vendor to company site

If company owned equipment was used, the Work Accomplished code (Code Key 15) would be "20 - Towing" and the Repair Site code (Code Key 17) would be "1 - Facility." The appropriate "Reason for Repair" (Code Key 14) and "Repair Class" (Code Key 16) would also be checked.

Again, the appropriate "Reason for Repair" (Code Key 14) and "Repair Class" (Code Key 16) would be checked.

It is not necessary to enter specific systems data in the Labor Information portion of the Repair Order prepared for towing.

Upon completion of the towing, the related Repair Order form is completed. A subsequent repair order is then prepared to accomplish required repairs or maintenance to the equipment.

#### Special Instructions for Completing the Repair Order Form (Form 6) For Completing Warranty Work

Properly prepared Repair Order forms are an excellent data source for the support and documentation of warranty claims and for the development of warranty information systems files.

When known warranty work is to be performed on a piece of equipment, a repair order should be prepared with the Reason for Repair (code key 14) "38, Warranty." No other work should be performed on the equipment and recorded the repair order. This requirement allows easy identification of warranty data within a maintenance information system.

The mechanic should perform the prescribed work on the piece of equipment and complete

the Mechanic's Copy as described previously. It may be necessary to assist the parts specialist in identifying and coding specific data in the their completion of the parts information portion of the repair order.

The parts specialist will issue the necessary parts and enter the appropriate parts data in accordance with Repair Order form Instructions 29-35. In entering the parts data it is essential that only the part failure code for the specific part that failed under warranty be entered. No parts failure code should be entered for additional parts which had to be replaced as a result of the failed part.

If, in the course of work, a potential warranty situation is identified, a separate Repair Order form should be prepared and the required warranty repair performed under this repair order as outline previously. Similarly, if in the process of reviewing the repair order, the supervisor or inspector notes work should be categorized as "38, Warranty," a new repair order should be prepared for such work under the warranty classification. The applicable parts, parts failure, and labor data should be deleted from all copies of the first Repair Order form and transferred to the new "Warranty" Repair Order form.

NOTE: In the development and functioning of maintenance management information systems which identify warranty data, it is essential that in the subsequent settlement of warranty claims the fact of the claim not be eliminated. Full or partial cost figures may be adjusted, but complete removal of the claim identity will distort future evaluations and comparisons of pieces of equipment, their specifications, components and manufacturer performance in claims adjustments.

# Instructions for Completing The VMRS Equipment File (*Form 30*)

#### Introduction

The VMRS<sup>™</sup> Equipment File (*Form 30*) satisfies two basic requirements for efficient fleet management.

- It provides a simple and effective means of ensuring compliance with Section 396.2 of the Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Regulations.
- It enhances both daily and long-term fleet maintenance operations and record keeping requirements by providing:
  - a descriptive ready reference of each unit, its major components and service function within the fleet.
  - a visual record of the maintenance history of the unit.
  - a means of filing, in a single location and safeguarding, all pertinent records of the unit required by both regulatory agencies, as well as the demands of effective fleet management.

A VMRS Equipment File should be established for each major unit of fleet equipment at the time of acquisition or entry into service. Furthermore, no maintenance service— or repair order — should begin on a piece of equipment until the shop foreman checks the equipment's history. Equipment files should be readily accessible to persons responsible for preparing repair orders for this very purpose.

In cases where multiple maintenance facilities require centralized record keeping, these files provide a ready means for semi-skilled persons to initially identify repetitive work on the same equipment system. In such cases, the complete file being brought to the prompt attention of technically qualified persons may result in significant financial savings.

While designed for use in conjunction with VMRS, the VMRS Equipment File has proven itself to be highly effective in its use by fleets which have not yet implemented VMRS.

### Establishing VMRS Equipment Files

#### 1. General

Enter that information which best suits the nature of your operation and the requirements of Section 396.2 of the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations.

NOTE: The VMRS Equipment File is designed for records of power units—mainly trucks and tractors. However, it may also be effectively used for keeping records on other powered and non-powered pieces of equipment.

Use of this file will enable all levels of management to have a quick, visible profile of the maintenance service record of each unit maintained. It is not intended that this file replace the detailed maintenance information obtainable from maintenance information systems. This file is principally for shop level use with domiciled equipment. It may also be of value in central maintenance file systems.

The contents of this File should contain the following items:

- Equipment Master Record, line set tickets, "Spec" sheets, or similar documents which identify or describe the equipment and its components.
- All repair orders written (maintenance and mechanic's copy), including records of lubrication and inspection.
- All vendor invoices received (originals or copies).
- All driver reports received (originals or copies).
- All Tire Records maintained.

#### 2. Preparation and Use

A. Enter required information on front and inside back covers.

- Unless a significant difference exists, the "Acquired" date and the date-in-service of the unit should be considered the same.
- "Assignment" and "Location" should be kept current. Changes in status from Linehaul to P&D, etc., should be made whenever occurring.
- Enter "Beginning" miles or hours as of the date the equipment was acquired. Do not record meter readings in tenths.
- Enter appropriate detail for major compo-

nents in the space provided. Entries in the "Beginning" column should be updated when components are changed. This will provide a source of life-to-date information.

B. Entries reflecting service performed should be at time the repair order is filed.

- Use one line for each repair order (or repair order line if desired), entering repair order date, R. O. number, and meter reading (hours or miles) in the columns provided, then insert the repair order in the Equipment File folder.
- Enter record of PMs, lubrications, or inspections in the PM column in accordance with the appropriate Work Accomplished code (code key 15).
- Enter the appropriate Work Accomplished code (code key 15) in the column beneath the system on which work was preformed. Alternatively, if the Work Accomplished code is not known or used at all simply enter an "X" in the column and/or the total dollars expended on each system for parts and labor to provide a basic equipment maintenance cost record. If a pre-headed column is not provided for the system worked on, use the "Other" column.
- The "Remarks or Total Dollars" column is intended for brief explanations and notes. If cost figures are posted the total dollars from the repair order may be entered in this column.

C. As work is done continue entering maintenance service records on outside back of the Equipment File and then inside the front flap. Insert continuation sheets (VMRS *Form 31*) as needed.

# Instructions for Completing The VMRS Repair Order Log (*Form 20/21*)

#### Introduction

The VMRS<sup>™</sup> Repair Order Log (Form 20) provides fleet management with a way to help control and measure performance within its maintenance shop operations. It provides maintenance supervisors with a simple, consolidated record of repair orders written and their status (i.e., open, completed, work delayed, etc.). The second copy of the log may be used as an analysis tool of shop operations and as a data entry form for maintenance management information systems. The subsequent processing of this data can provide management with meaningful information as described further in this section. With this information at hand, supervisors can better maintain control of the assignment of the work force and the allocation of the available work space.

The one-part VMRS Repair Order Log (*Form 21*) can be used in the maintenance shop to assist shop foremen and shift supervisors in scheduling work priority, ensuring continuity of work from shift-to-shift, responding to inquires as to the availability of equipment, and aiding in monitoring the status of work in progress. When delays occur, they should be entered in the log so that corrective action can be taken, responsible managers notified, and scheduling adjustments made to keep the maintenance facility operating efficiently.

Data contained in properly completed Repair Order Logs include:

- Identity of maintenance facility.
- Period covered.
- Equipment type and ID number.
- Repair Order Number.
- Reason for Repair (code key 14).
- Repair Class (code key 16).
- Repair Site (code key 17).
- Date and hour the equipment was received by the maintenance department.
- Date and hour the maintenance department scheduled the return of the equipment to service.
- Primary reason for any delays in meeting scheduled "return to service" time.
- Actual date and hour the equipment is

returned to service.

#### Using the Repair Order Log

Data obtained from the Repair Order Log and other maintenance data sources should be an integral part of any fleet maintenance management information system.

The primary purpose of analyzing data from shop logs is to identify shop locations having difficulty completing their jobs on schedule. It can also aid in determining the causes of time overruns so appropriate corrective actions can be taken. Bear in mind, schedule completion times may be unrealistic due to pressures from outside the shop or of ineffective estimating techniques. An appropriate management level should consider evidence that such problems exist and take steps to speed improvements.

If the scheduled out times are realistic, failure analysis will highlight problems which interfere with shop and equipment operations productivity. Investments in increased parts inventory, or additional mechanics, space, or tools may be justifiable. If the resources are available, supervision and management functions may need improvement. This section provides several samples of reports which can be developed from Repair Order Logs.

**Report 7-1**, "Analysis of Maintenance Scheduling/Performance" identifies and compares the validity of shop scheduling forecasts vs. shop performance. The value of the report is increased when job standards are used in the shop work scheduling process. Because of shop organization and equipment types, it may be advisable to prepare this report in several categories, such as "Power Units—Linehaul."

**Report 7-2**, "Reasons for Delay, By Facility" identifies the causes of work not being completed within acceptable norms or established standards. Management must determine corrective measures (i.e., distributing work better between under-used and overworked shops).

**Report 7-3**, "Reason for Delay, By Facility, and Shift, Power Units" identifies the causes of work not being completed within acceptable norms or established standards. This report provides detail not contained in Report 7-2 and is especially useful in multi-shift shop operations.

#### Summary

The VMRS Repair Order Log assists in organizing work at hand and/or anticipated work. When used in a multi-shift operation, the Log enables each oncoming shift supervisor to quickly gain an understanding of the continuing as well as incoming work load. This permits the orderly assignment of work based on known priorities and available labor.

The Repair Order Log, like the Repair Order form, is a valuable source of data for the development of maintenance management information reports. For these purposes, the two-part Repair Order Log (*Form 20*) should be used.
